Why this challenge

No single foreign kill switch over India's connectivity

Satellite internet can reach every village, ship and disaster zone. But when one operator controls the satellites and the software that runs them, the country using it does not control whether the service keeps running. Recent events show this is a real risk, not a theory.

What the record shows

What this means for India

India has kept independent policy choices under outside pressure. Its critical communications should never become a lever that someone else can pull.

Routing Indian traffic through gateways in India is necessary, but it is not enough: it decides where traffic lands, not whether the satellites keep serving India. A trusted design also needs local survivability, fallback to Indian networks, more than one provider and, over time, Indian-controlled capacity.

The principle is country-neutral. It applies to any foreign provider, whatever its nationality. Companies that want to serve India are welcome, on terms that put India's security first.

Priority order for every entry. 1. India's security and control of its own connectivity: a pass/fail gate. 2. Then privacy, resilience, cost and fair competition, judged within that limit.

The challenge

Trusted Satellite Internet Protocol Challenge

Design a protocol or architecture that protects India's networks while keeping competition and consumer choice. Judging looks at the design, not at the company or country behind any technology it is compared with.

Key dates

Dates may change. The rules page is the reference.

Tracks

  • Student: undergraduate and postgraduate teams of 1–4
  • Engineer: professionals and researchers, teams of 1–4
  • Policy and governance: legal, audit and accountability designs; no code required
  • Bonus prize: best post-quantum-ready key management design

A valid entry contains

  • Must pass the sovereignty gate in App 3: India keeps control of service continuity, jurisdiction and dependence on any single provider
  • A design of 6 pages or fewer: the protocol, the threats it addresses and the threats it leaves open
  • The JSON file exported from App 3, or an equivalent simulation or code
  • A map to the 17 threats, with one stated trade-off per threat
  • Optional: a 3-minute video for the finale
